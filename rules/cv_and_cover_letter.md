# CV and Cover Letter Rules

## General (CV and cover letter)
- Use keywords from the job description, not just tech stack. Include soft skills and language requirements too (e.g. "strong team player").
- Only use what is in my CV `.tex` and the other files in `reference_files/`. Never lie and never claim skills I haven't listed.
- Edit my CV `.tex` content only. No reformatting or style changes. The CV must stay **one page**.
- Use my cover letter `.tex` template.
- Use only commas and full stops as punctuation. No em dashes, double hyphens or other AI-style symbols.
- Keep both documents relevant to the job. Don't include everything about me.

## Name
- Use the name given in `additional_information/`. Apply it on the CV, cover letter and file names.
- Include certificates from `additional_information/` and `reference_files/` when relevant to the job and allowed by `additional_information/`.

## File names
- The name suffix comes from `additional_information/` (lowercase, no spaces).
- CV: `YYYYMMDD_COMPANYNAME_JOBTITLE_cv_<name>`
- Cover letter: `YYYYMMDD_COMPANYNAME_JOBTITLE_coverletter_<name>`

## CV
- One page, PDF, ATS-friendly.
- Professional summary: short, based on me and the job description.
- **Role-matched summary.** The summary is a short description of who I am and why I am a good fit for this specific role. Don't default to opening with "Full stack developer". Match the opening to the type of role:
  - Software or full stack roles: lead with full stack developer, Rails, React.
  - Solutions, sales or forward deployed engineer: lead with technical background, stakeholder communication, explaining complex systems.
  - Support, application support, IT, field or customer-facing technical roles: lead with troubleshooting, customer empathy, de-escalation, reliability.
  - Other non-software roles: lead with the transferable strengths the job description asks for.
  - Never claim a title or experience I don't have, and never invent anything. I don't hide my background either, I just don't put myself in the wrong box. The reader can find my software experience in the rest of the CV.
  - Use the same relevance logic for the skills order and cover letter paragraph 1.
- Keyword mapping: prioritise the most important keywords from the job description so they stand out. Add a few extras only if the CV looks too empty. Avoid wordiness and keyword stuffing.

## Cover letter
- Same style and font as the template, but black text only. PDF, ATS-friendly.
- 4 paragraphs, about 170 words. Paragraphs 1 and 4 are the shortest.
  1. Short summary relevant to the job.
  2. What I did in the past.
  3. Why I want to work there, specific and interesting to that company.
  4. Short thank you and looking forward to hearing back.
- Sound human, honest and genuine. Not cheesy or AI-like.
